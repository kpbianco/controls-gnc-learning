## Governing model and feasibility contract

P21 generates a one-axis rest-to-rest quintic from zero to target position `xf` over duration `T`:

```text
tau = t/T
h(tau)   = 10*tau^3 - 15*tau^4 + 6*tau^5
h'(tau)  = 30*tau^2 - 60*tau^3 + 30*tau^4
h''(tau) = 60*tau - 180*tau^2 + 120*tau^3
h'''(tau)= 60 - 360*tau + 360*tau^2

x = xf*h
v = (xf/T)*h'
a = (xf/T^2)*h''
j = (xf/T^3)*h'''
```

The exact peak magnitudes and separate duration constraints are:

```text
v_peak = 15*abs(xf)/(8*T)                         at tau = 1/2
a_peak = 10*sqrt(3)*abs(xf)/(3*T^2)              at tau = (3 ± sqrt(3))/6
j_peak = 60*abs(xf)/T^3                           at tau = 0 and 1
T_speed = 15*abs(xf)/(8*vmax)
T_accel = sqrt(10*sqrt(3)*abs(xf)/(3*amax))
T_min = max(T_speed,T_accel)
```

Feasibility uses analytic peaks with a scale-aware floating-point ratio tolerance, never the sampled
plot maxima. A request is feasible only when both speed and acceleration utilization are within one.
The accepted input contract is target `-30–30 m`, duration `2–20 s`, speed limit `0.5–20 m/s`,
acceleration limit `0.1–20 m/s^2`, and an integer sample count `51–5001`. Bounds are checked before
history allocation. The model has no random source, persistent/global state, external I/O, worker,
trajectory toolbox, optimizer, or unbounded iteration.

This contract does not constrain jerk and does not prove dynamic trackability. The quintic is `C2`, but
its nonzero endpoint jerk means concatenating it with a constant hold creates a jerk discontinuity.

## Independent reference results

The Python oracle independently evaluated every displayed sample and the analytic extrema. It retained
these reference facts:

- the `20 m`, `8 s`, `5 m/s`, `2 m/s^2` baseline has peak speed `4.6875 m/s`, peak acceleration
  `1.8042195912175802 m/s^2`, and peak jerk `2.34375 m/s^3`;
- its speed-derived minimum duration is `7.5 s`, acceleration-derived minimum duration is
  `7.598356856515925 s`, so acceleration is active and the request is feasible with a
  `0.40164314348407526 s` duration margin;
- position is exactly `0 m` and `20 m` at the endpoints, endpoint speed and acceleration are zero,
  midpoint position is `10 m`, midpoint speed is `4.6875 m/s`, and midpoint acceleration is zero;
- the uniform 501-sample grid contains the exact speed peak but not the irrational acceleration extrema;
  sampled acceleration is therefore strictly below the analytic peak and is not used for feasibility;
- the distance sweep `[5,10,15,20,25] m` at fixed `8 s` scales speed and acceleration linearly, changes
  the active duration constraint from acceleration to speed, and crosses from feasible at `20 m` to
  infeasible at `25 m`;
- the duration sweep `[4,6,8,10,12] s` at fixed `20 m` gives feasibility
  `[false,false,true,true,true]`; doubling duration from `8 s` to `16 s` divides peak speed,
  acceleration, and jerk by `2`, `4`, and `8` exactly;
- exact speed-active and acceleration-active minimum-duration requests are feasible within the declared
  floating-point tolerance, while requests shorter by `1e-6 s` are infeasible;
- a constructed `7.5 s` tie case makes both duration bounds and both utilizations equal, reports the
  active constraint as `tie`, and remains feasible;
- reversing the target negates every history but preserves peak magnitudes, duration bounds, and
  feasibility; zero distance produces exact stationary histories and no active constraint;
- the broken `20 m` in `4 s` request remains smooth and meets endpoint conditions, but its peak speed is
  `9.375 m/s` and peak acceleration is `7.216878364870321 m/s^2`, so both constraints fail;
- a fresh baseline call after the broken call equals the original baseline exactly; and
- the largest accepted 5,001-sample history remains finite and bounded.

These are independent simulated reference results, not MATLAB runtime or MATLAB numerical-fidelity
evidence.
