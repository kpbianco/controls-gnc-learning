## Governing model and robust-design contract

P20 retains P19's exact zero-order-held first-order speed plant:

```text
alpha = exp(-a*dt)
beta  = (b/a)*(1-alpha)
v[k+1] = alpha*v[k] + beta*u[k]

nominal: u_N[k] = (a0/b0)*r[k] + K_N*(r[k]-v_N[k])
robust:  u_R[k] = Kp*(r[k]-v_R[k]) + Ki*z[k]
         z[k+1] = z[k] + dt*(r[k]-v_R[k])
```

The positive design grid is actuator gain ratio `[0.5,0.75,1,1.25,1.5]` crossed with drag
ratio `[0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2]`. The finite PI candidate set crosses `Kp=[0.5,1,1.5,2] 1/s`
with `Ki=[0.4,0.8,1.2] 1/s^2`. Every candidate is evaluated for 12 seconds at `dt=0.02 s`
on every grid point using a `1 m/s` step. A candidate is feasible only if both explicit quadratic roots remain inside
the unit circle for every scenario and its maximum squared-command integral is at most
`90 m^2/s^3`. The feasible candidate with smallest worst tracking ISE wins; exact ties preserve the
declared lower-`Kp`, then lower-`Ki` enumeration order.

Changing the interactive reference between `0` and `2 m/s` is exploratory. The selected gains remain
fixed, but the `90 m^2/s^3` effort guarantee applies only to the `1 m/s`, 12-second,
`dt=0.02 s` design conditions.

The search owns 12 candidates, 25 scenarios, 600 transitions per evaluation, and an exact bound of
180,000 design transitions per call. The user simulation is separately bounded at 5,000 transitions
and terminates a divergent trace if speed exceeds `1,000 m/s`. No random source, toolbox synthesis,
background worker, persistent model state, external I/O, or unbounded search is used.

## Independent reference results

The Python oracle independently propagated both controllers and repeated the complete candidate
search. It retained these reference facts:

- the selected robust candidate is `Kp=2 1/s`, `Ki=0.8 1/s^2`;
- its worst-grid tracking ISE is `1.76331826901075 m^2/s` and worst command-effort integral is
  `84.54677730094271 m^2/s^3`, below the declared limit;
- at the matched plant, nominal ISE is `0.20713356588115908 m^2/s` and robust ISE is
  `0.38021285022257995 m^2/s`, so the nominal design wins its stated operating-point metric;
- matched nominal and robust command-effort integrals are `12.678090522965032` and
  `10.755342628367382 m^2/s^3`, respectively; no single design is claimed to dominate every metric;
- at the weak-actuator/high-drag corner, nominal and robust ISE are
  `3.4960569186739505` and `1.76331826901075 m^2/s`; robust effort is higher there but remains
  below its selection constraint;
- the nominal worst-corner equilibrium is `5/11 m/s`, whereas stable positive-plant PI has exact
  equilibrium speed `1 m/s` and required command `4 m/s^2`; its finite 12-second final error remains
  above `0.14 m/s`, so equilibrium and finite-horizon claims stay distinct;
- both five-point sweeps reset every non-swept input and show crossing ISE curves: nominal is better
  at the matched point while robust is better at several uncertainty points;
- equal actuator and drag ratios preserve the nominal feedforward equilibrium, and zero reference
  from rest gives exact zero histories, zero tracking ISE, zero command effort, and no divergence for
  both designs at every exposed positive or reversed-sign plant setting;
- reversed actuator polarity gives both controllers pole magnitude above one and triggers bounded
  early termination; a fresh positive-polarity call exactly recovers the baseline;
- the largest accepted 5,001-sample positive-polarity reference runs remain finite and stable.

These are independent simulated reference results, not MATLAB runtime or MATLAB numerical-fidelity
evidence. The finite grid does not establish behavior between grid points or outside its bounds.
